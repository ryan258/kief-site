## What the DM has given us

Candy Land, long after the story ended well: a kingdom warped and gnarled by a curse, a King missing for years, a Princess rumored kidnapped, dangerous roads, and no clear authority. The party has a map, a trail of half-forgotten stories, and no promise of a happy ending.

This earlier pitch establishes the setting, not gear or party composition. The DM’s later wit-and-whimsy direction and spell-use guidance are recorded above; the following are interpretations of the premise.

- **Travel is content.** Dangerous roads mean repeated overland trouble rather than one dungeon. Mr. Big as point cow, Fly, Feather Fall, Misty Step, and a day's slots kept in reserve matter more than usual.
- **The map is the plot.** Expect navigation, landmarks, and places where the map is wrong. Speak with Animals and ritual Detect Magic are the cheap investigative tools; neither reads a curse's terms.
- **A curse needs rules before it can be fought.** Detect Magic reports that something is magical, not what the curse does or how to end it. Kief has no Remove Curse and no healing; ask the DM how curses resolve at this table.
- **Check condition immunities on the actual stat block.** *Hypnotic Pattern* relies on Charmed; *Fear* applies Frightened and also drops items on a failed save. Creature type alone does not grant these immunities, and immunity to Frightened does not erase the spell's separate dropped-item text. Web, Fireball, and 2024 Command have different requirements; assess each spell individually.

## Confirmed DM ruling — Find Familiar

Recorded 2026-10-07 from Binyled's message relayed by Ryan (message timestamp 11:00 AM; original message date not supplied).

- The familiar is an actual physical creature, not a disposable summoned spirit.
- You can summon it and put it away. Once summoned, that individual is your familiar.
- If it dies, it is gone. Do not plan on restoring it by recasting *Find Familiar*.
- Its bond to you means its death negatively affects you.
- The DM considers it intelligent and able to provide legitimate help. It will not willingly sacrifice itself just because it is asked.

**At the table:** treat Mr. Big as a companion with judgment and self-preservation. Agree on a safe job and an exit; stop or change the plan when he refuses.
