## What the DM has given us

Candy Land, long after the story ended well: a kingdom warped and gnarled by a curse, a King missing for years, a Princess rumored kidnapped, dangerous roads, and no clear authority. The party has a map, a trail of half-forgotten stories, and no promise of a happy ending.

This is premise, not table rules. It tells us what the campaign will ask of Kief; it establishes nothing about gear, party composition, or DM policy.

- **Travel is content.** Dangerous roads mean repeated overland trouble rather than one dungeon. Mr. Big as point cow, Fly, Feather Fall, Misty Step, and a day's slots kept in reserve matter more than usual.
- **The map is the plot.** Expect navigation, landmarks, and places where the map is wrong. Speak with Animals and ritual Detect Magic are the cheap investigative tools; neither reads a curse's terms.
- **A curse needs rules before it can be fought.** Detect Magic reports that something is magical, not what the curse does or how to end it. Kief has no Remove Curse and no healing; ask the DM how curses resolve at this table.
- **Check condition immunities on the actual stat block.** *Hypnotic Pattern* relies on Charmed; *Fear* applies Frightened and also drops items on a failed save. Creature type alone does not grant these immunities, and immunity to Frightened does not erase the spell's separate dropped-item text. Web, Fireball, and 2024 Command have different requirements; assess each spell individually.

## Still unknown

- Actual D&D Beyond level, spell selections, inventory, and campaign linkage. No live interaction was performed.
- Campaign ruleset. The documented build is 2024 PHB; the premise itself does not establish that the new DM uses those rules.
- New DM's starting gear, gold, magic-item allowance, fixed-HP policy, and familiar reskin approval.
- Fey is a legal familiar spirit type under *Find Familiar*; only the cosmetic miniature-cow appearance needs DM approval. Live familiar entry selection remains unverified; the intended current Cat block is linked and recorded locally.
- Party composition. The spell choices emphasize broad teammate utility; more specific optimization can follow once roles are known.
- How the curse resolves: a targeted effect that Remove Curse or Dispel Magic can touch, a regional condition, or plot-only. No build choice here answers it.
- Travel rules in use — exhaustion, rations, random encounters, downtime between legs of the map — and whether the party starts already travelling together.
- Where the map came from and what Kief's own stake in the fallen kingdom is. A tea merchant has an obvious commercial interest in a sugar kingdom; the DM may want a better reason.
- Tone limits. The pitch promises a dark fairy tale; confirm how grim the table wants the material before leaning on it.
