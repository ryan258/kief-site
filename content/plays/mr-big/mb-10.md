In the street, he's a stray something. Strays are easy to ignore.

### When to use it

- Urban scouting in daylight, where a hooded gnome draws eyes and a small
  animal draws shrugs.
- Following, loitering, and casing in populated places.

### Mechanics

1. Towns ignore small animals. Mr. Big at street level (Speed 40, gutter and
   cart-shadow routes) usually reads as someone's odd pet.
2. Children look, follow, and grab ([play 64](../mb-64/)). Route around
   them.
3. Guards examine wagons and papers, not animals. He carries nothing that
   would make him a story.
4. Within 100 feet, reports are live. Beyond it, standing orders
   ([play 90](../mb-90/)).
5. A tiny *cow* is odder than a cat. The DM decides how much attention the
   cosmetic form draws.

### At the table

Mechanically: "Mr. Big shadows the courier from street level: forty feet of
speed, Stealth plus four when it matters. I trail a block behind, inside a
hundred feet."

Verbally: *"Half the market sees the tiny cow and smiles. None of them see
him again ten seconds later."*

### The rule underneath

There's no "blend into a crowd" subsystem: plausibility is the mechanic,
and the DM decides what draws attention before any dice. Stealth is for
avoiding notice; a creature that is noticed but dismissed never needed the
roll.
