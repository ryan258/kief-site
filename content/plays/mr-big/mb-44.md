Enemies who chase the cow are giving the party their actions.

### When to use it

- Never on purpose in melee. When an enemy **chooses** to hunt Mr. Big, the
  party exploits it.

### Mechanics

1. His survival plan is to be expensive to chase: up the wall (Climb 40),
   under furniture, out the window.
2. Party doctrine: punish the distraction. Kief's
   [Ray of Frost](../../kief/k-8/) slows the pursuer; allies attack its
   exposed back.
3. If he's cornered, Kief can **dismiss him to the pocket dimension** as a
   Magic action ([play 85](../mb-85/)).
4. This isn't bait. Deliberate luring is [play 48](../mb-48/).

### At the table

Mechanically: "The gnoll's chasing Mr. Big? He's up the buttress, Climb 40,
and the gnoll's back is to Sable. If it corners him, dismissing him is one
Magic action."

Verbally: *"The gnoll invests its whole magnificent fury in a creature the
size of a boot, who ascends the wall like spilled water running backward."*

### The rule underneath

Opportunity Attacks trigger when a creature leaves your reach using its
movement. Each enemy gets one action per round; anything that makes it
spend that action on a 2-HP decoy is tempo for the party. Action economy is
the real hit point total of an encounter.
