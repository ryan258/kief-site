Kief defers hard calls to the cow. The table gets a bit and, sometimes, a
real report.

### When to use it

- A tough social question or a suspicious offer, where a pause helps the
  scene.
- When unsettling the other side of the table has value.
- Never to override the party's decision. When lives are at stake, Kief
  listens to the party.

### Mechanics

1. The bit: Kief turns to Mr. Big, waits, interprets the blink. One blink
   yes, two no, a slow stare means the question was beneath him.
2. The real part: Mr. Big may have noticed something (Perception +3), and
   telepathy delivers it. The blink Kief "interprets" can carry a genuine
   report.
3. The flaw, honestly played: Kief overstates how much Mr. Big understands.
   The cow's opinion is **flavor, not a veto**.
4. Zero cost, always available.

### At the table

Mechanically: "Before I answer the baron, I consult Mr. Big. …Two blinks.
That's a no. Separately, Insight plus three: does the baron believe his own
story?"

Verbally: *"The gnome and the cow confer in perfect silence. 'Mr. Big has
concerns,' Kief reports at last, and somehow this lands harder than any
accusation in the room."*

### The rule underneath

Not everything needs mechanics: the DM adjudicates social fiction directly.
Where dice enter, reading a person is Wisdom (Insight), and it's Kief's
roll, not the cow's. A character quirk works best when it adds play without
taking decisions away from anyone else at the table.
