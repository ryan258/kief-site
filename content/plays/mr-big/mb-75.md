Suspected pressure-plate floor, crossed first by the party's lightest
member, **if the DM says his weight won't set it off**.

### When to use it

- The party has already found signs of mechanical pressure plates and wants
  a safe line through them.

### Mechanics

1. **Ask first**: "does a creature of a few pounds trigger it?" Traps do what
   the DM says they detect.
2. A no-trigger crossing maps a line, nothing more. It doesn't find or disarm
   anything.
3. **Not** for magical triggers, glyphs, or anything whose trigger is
   unknown. Those are [Detect Magic](../../kief/k-34/) and the party's
   trap specialist.
4. The party still crosses carefully, with its best searcher working the
   floor.
5. At 2 HP, any trap that does fire probably ends the form
   ([play 52](../mb-52/)).

### At the table

Mechanically: "We've found plates in this corridor. Would a creature Mr.
Big's size set one off? If not, he walks the edge slowly and we follow his
line. If you're not sure, he doesn't go."

Verbally: *"The corridor's designers budgeted for greed arriving at two
hundred pounds. Greed's advance scout arrives at a few, and walks very
carefully."*

### The rule underneath

Traps are DM adjudication: the DM decides what a trap detects and what beats
it. Asking about a specific method beats rolling a generic check. Checks
belong to whoever is trained; the cow scouts, but disarming stays with the
proficient.
