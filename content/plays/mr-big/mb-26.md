Tiny opens routes nobody else in the party can use.

### When to use it

- A vent, a drain grate, a gap under the floor, a hole in the wall.
- The party needs to know what's on the other side before it can decide how
  to get there.

### Mechanics

1. A Tiny creature fits through gaps sized for a Tiny creature. Squeezing
   through a smaller space costs extra movement.
2. On the far side, he can **look, listen, and report**. He usually can't
   open a lock, lift a bar, or turn a heavy handle; the DM decides what a
   cat can manage.
3. Telepathy passes through walls, within 100 feet.
4. A dead end in a crawlspace is a trap for a 2-HP creature. Don't send him
   where he can't turn around.

### At the table

Mechanically: "Mr. Big goes through the drain grate into the courtyard. I
want to know what's on the other side of the gate, and whether the bar is
the only thing holding it."

Verbally: *"The fortress was designed to stop armies. The drain was designed
to stop rain. Neither was designed with a cow in mind."*

### The rule underneath

Size determines space and what gaps a creature fits through. A creature can
squeeze into a space sized for one size smaller at extra movement cost.
Telepathy has a range, not a line-of-sight requirement, so walls don't block
reports.
