A light line, carried where nobody else can climb.

### When to use it

- Vertical or gap problems where the **first** crossing is the hard one.

### Mechanics

1. A climbing rope is too heavy for a Tiny cat on a long climb. Stage it:
   Mr. Big carries **light twine** up (Climb 40); the party hauls the rope up
   on the twine; a climber follows.
2. He **can't tie knots**. A loop dropped over a rock horn is clever and
   DM-adjudicated. A person rigs the real anchor.
3. Never trust a person's weight to a cow-placed line.
4. Rope and twine are **pending purchase**.
5. Alternative: Kief's [Fly and rope](../../kief/k-36/), at the cost of a
   3rd-level slot.

### At the table

Mechanically: "Nobody can reach that shelf, but he can. Mr. Big takes the
twine up the face and around the rock horn. We pull the rope up on it,
Brenna climbs it with us holding the end, and she ties the anchor for
everyone else."

Verbally: *"The cliff has stopped four expeditions. It has no policy at all
for something that walks up it trailing a length of string."*

### The rule underneath

Without a climb speed, climbing costs extra movement and hard surfaces need
Athletics checks; with one, you just move. Carrying capacity is Strength
score × 15 pounds, halved for Tiny. Mr. Big's is about 22, but awkward loads
on a climb are DM territory. Stage loads small.
