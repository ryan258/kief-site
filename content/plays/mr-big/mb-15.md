Exact numbers before the party bets its life on them.

### When to use it

- The last check before any ambush, assault, or *Fireball*. Plans built on
  "about six" fail to the seventh.

### Mechanics

1. Mr. Big circles the group (camp, room, or column) at Stealth +4, from
   ankle height or rooftop.
2. Kief does the counting through shared senses ([play 2](../mb-2/)). A cat
   can report "many"; Kief reports "nine."
3. Two looks from different angles: eyes miss what's behind the wagon.
   Listen for voices that don't match visible bodies.
4. The report includes disposition: armed, armored, alert, asleep, and
   who's giving orders.
5. Feed it to Kief's targeting: cluster size for [Fireball](../../kief/k-1/)
   or [Pattern](../../kief/k-2/), the leader for *Command*.

### At the table

Mechanically: "Before we hit the camp: Mr. Big circles it, and I look
through his eyes from the far side too. If I hear more voices than I can
see people, I want to know where the difference is."

Verbally: *"'Nine,' Kief says at last. 'You can see eight. The ninth is in
the wagon, and he's the one everyone keeps glancing at.'"*

### The rule underneath

Hidden creatures aren't erased. Stealth beats sight, but sound gives the DM
grounds to reveal presence without position. For area spells, exact
numbers matter mechanically: a 20-foot radius either catches the seventh
enemy or it doesn't.
