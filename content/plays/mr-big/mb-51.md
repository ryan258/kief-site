Covering the retreat: the last one out is the one nobody counts.

### When to use it

- The party is disengaging and needs to know whether pursuit follows.

### Mechanics

1. Mr. Big takes a high or hidden vantage over the withdrawal route and
   watches: who follows, how many, gaining or not.
2. He hops vantage to vantage behind the party, staying within 100 feet of
   Kief.
3. His report prices the options: [Ray of Frost](../../kief/k-8/) the lead
   pursuer, [Web](../../kief/k-25/) behind, or stop running because nobody's
   chasing.
4. Last to leave, and hard to catch: Stealth +4, Climb 40, and pursuers are
   watching the party.
5. If he falls behind the range, Kief can dismiss and recall him once the
   party stops ([play 85](../mb-85/)).

### At the table

Mechanically: "We break contact. Mr. Big takes the wall-top behind us and
tells me who's following. If it's the two fast ones, I've got a Web for
that archway. If it's nobody, we walk."

Verbally: *"Above and behind the fleeing party, hopping cornice to cornice
like a rumor, the smallest rearguard in military history keeps the books on
who is brave enough to follow."*

### The rule underneath

Chases resolve with ordinary movement: compare speeds, spend Dash actions,
apply terrain. A pursuer who can't out-move you can't catch you, which
makes a rear observer's report a complete tactical answer. The cheap
counters are movement math: speed reduction, difficult terrain, or an
obstacle.
