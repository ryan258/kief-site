When telepathy runs out, paper doesn't.

### When to use it

- The party is split beyond 100 feet: across a siege, a district, a valley.

### Mechanics

1. A short note, written by Kief (Calligrapher's Supplies proficiency; paper
   and a small tie or pouch **pending purchase**), fastened where Mr. Big can
   carry it. Ask the DM what a Tiny cat can reasonably carry.
2. He runs it at Speed 40, Climb 40, along wall-tops and hedgerows.
3. He can't read or speak and can't be questioned. The note can still be
   read if he's caught: write as if it will be.
4. The recipient must know to write a reply and tie it back on. Brief allies
   **before** the split.
5. He needs clear, simple instructions for where to go
   ([play 90](../mb-90/)). A cat finds Kief or a known person more reliably
   than an address.

### At the table

Mechanically: "Brenna's team is four hundred feet away across the yard.
Note on the harness: 'gate at the third bell.' Mr. Big takes the wall-top
route to Brenna. Reply comes back the same way."

Verbally: *"The siege lines are watched by a hundred archers. Between them,
along the broken wall, trots the only courier in the war that both sides
have decided isn't real."*

### The rule underneath

Travel math: a creature covers its speed per round, about 10 times its speed
in feet per minute, and Dash doubles movement for a round. Nothing requires
a courier to be a person; any obedient creature can carry an object it can
bear. When the problem is "move X to Y," check movement before spells.
