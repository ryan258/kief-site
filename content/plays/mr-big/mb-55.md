Where the keys live, and when nobody's holding them.

### When to use it

- Any lock the party intends to be on the other side of.

### Mechanics

1. Keys follow habits: they hang on hooks, ride belts, change hands on
   schedule. Mr. Big watches the lock ([play 18](../mb-18/)); Kief looks
   through him at the moments that matter.
2. The deliverable is a key map: which ring, whose belt, where it hangs at
   night, when it's unattended.
3. Cow finds, party takes. An unattended ring on a hook is Kief's
   [Mage Hand](../../kief/k-12/) from 30 feet. A worn ring is Sleight of
   Hand for someone trained.
4. He doesn't steal keys himself. A cow running off with a keyring is loud in
   the way that matters.

### At the table

Mechanically: "Mr. Big spends the afternoon on the watch-house roof beam. By
dusk I want to know which ring opens the cells, who carries it, and where it
hangs at night."

Verbally: *"The turnkey has carried the ring for nine years and never once
wondered who watches him hang it up."*

### The rule underneath

Objects are attended or unattended, and the rules treat them differently:
unattended objects can simply be taken or moved; attended ones require
Sleight of Hand against someone watching. Surveillance turns a contested
check into an uncontested one by finding the gap.
