Guard rotations, timed by the most patient observer available.

### When to use it

- An infiltration or ambush that depends on a gap in coverage.
- Confirming tonight's pattern matches last night's before the party bets on
  it.

### Mechanics

1. Post Mr. Big at a vantage with sight of the route ([play 18](../mb-18/)).
   He signals each pass; Kief counts the intervals.
2. Cats do stillness well. The DM decides when a watch needs a Stealth check
   and how often.
3. Kief does the counting. Mr. Big can signal "now" reliably; he can't
   report "every twelve minutes."
4. Beyond 100 feet, use a standing order ([play 90](../mb-90/)): watch the
   route, come back after the guard passes.

### At the table

Mechanically: "Mr. Big watches the gate from the woodpile for the next
hour. He pings me each time the guard passes, and I count the gap. When we
know it, we use it."

Verbally: *"The sentry walks his route eleven times. Eleven times, a small
shape in the woodpile declines to exist for him. The twelfth gap is the
party's."*

### The rule underneath

In 2024, a Hide check total becomes the DC for others to find you with
Perception, so a long watch isn't a new roll every minute. Hiding ends if
you're found, make noise, or attack. Time outside combat has no action
cost, so patience is free. Splitting the work (the cat watches, the gnome
counts) respects what each creature can actually do.
