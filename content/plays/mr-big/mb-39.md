The Help action in combat: real Advantage, spent rarely.

### When to use it

- The attack that decides the fight: the rogue's setup strike, the last chance
  at the fleeing boss.
- Not often. A combat Help attempt puts a fragile familiar next to the
  enemy.

### Mechanics

1. **Help** on his own turn: distract an enemy within **5 feet** of him. The
   next attack roll against it by one of his allies has **Advantage**,
   before the start of Mr. Big's next turn.
2. Initiative order matters: the ally's attack must come after Mr. Big's turn
   and before his next.
3. Exposure: 5 feet from something armed, at AC 12 and 2 HP. Arrive from a
   ledge or tabletop (Climb 40). He can move after helping, but Help uses his
   action, so there's no Dash away.
4. He can't attack. Help is his main contribution at melee range.

### At the table

Mechanically: "This is the moment. Mr. Big steps onto the mantel beside the
warlord. Help: Brenna's attack has Advantage, and she's next. Then he
climbs back up the chimney breast."

Verbally: *"The warlord's eyes flick to the impossible cow at his shoulder
for exactly one heartbeat, and Brenna's blade arrives in that heartbeat."*

### The rule underneath

The 2024 Help action (attack): distract an enemy within 5 feet of you, and
the next attack roll against it by an ally has Advantage before the start
of your next turn. Constraints (5 feet, next attack, expiry) dictate
position and initiative. Spending a 2-HP creature's turn on it is good math
only when the helped attack matters.
