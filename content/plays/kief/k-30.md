Two 3rd-level slots cover *Fireball*, *Hypnotic Pattern*, *Fear*, *Fly*,
and *Counterspell*. The discipline of keeping one back.

### When to use it

- A fight includes an enemy spellcaster, or might.
- Any time Kief is about to spend his second 3rd-level slot.

### Mechanics

1. At the start of the fight, decide: **is there a caster?** If yes, one
   3rd-level slot and Kief's Reaction belong to *Counterspell*
   ([play 4](../k-4/)) until that caster is handled.
2. The first slot can still go on control. *Hypnotic Pattern* may take the
   caster out of the fight without a counter.
3. Keep line of sight to the caster and stay within **60 feet** when
   feasible. A counter that is out of range doesn't exist.
4. The Reaction is shared. Choosing *Shield* against a sword blow means no
   *Counterspell* until Kief's next turn. Say which one you're guarding.
5. Emergency fallback: converting 5 SP into a 3rd-level slot is a Bonus
   Action on Kief's own turn. Plan it ahead; it can't be done in the middle
   of the enemy's turn.
6. No caster appears by the fight's end? The slot wasn't wasted. It's still
   there for the next fight.

### At the table

Mechanically: "Robed one at the back is a caster. I'm holding a 3rd-level
slot and my Reaction for her. Opening with Web, not Pattern, so I keep one
3rd. I'll stay inside 60 feet."

Verbally: *"Kief sets his cup down where he can reach it without looking,
and watches the robed figure's hands with the interest of a man who has
seen a great many people try to sell him something."*

### The rule underneath

Resources that can be spent in advance tempt you to spend them all at
once. A held slot is only valuable if the matching trigger can still
happen: sight, range, and an unused Reaction. Counterspell is also the one
spell that answers enemy magic from the enemy's turn, which is why the plan
has to exist before initiative, not after the enemy starts casting.
