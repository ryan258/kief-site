The two-turn trap: silk first, fire when the silk has done its work. Fire
destroys the webbing, so don't sabotage your own control casually.

### When to use it

- A chokepoint fight where holding the enemies first and ending them second
  is better than either alone.
- Both slots are affordable: a 2nd and one of two 3rds.

### Mechanics

1. **Turn 1**: [Web](../k-25/) across the chokepoint, allies outside. DC 14
   Dexterity save on first entry on a turn or at turn start, not immediately
   on casting; failure Restrains.
2. **Middle turns**: the party attacks Restrained targets with Advantage.
   Kief uses non-fire cantrips or Dodges. **No Fire Bolt into the web.**
3. **Closing turn**: allies clear the webbed area. Then **Fireball** into the
   cube: Restrained creatures make its Dexterity save at **Disadvantage**.
   8d6 fire.
4. The fire burns away the webs it touches. The Restrained condition ends
   with them, and a creature that starts its turn in a burning cube takes
   2d4 fire.
5. Order of operations is the whole play. Careful Spell makes chosen creatures
   succeed on a save; it doesn't fireproof them in burning webs.
6. Casting Fireball doesn't end Web by itself; the burn does. Kief's
   Concentration is free again once the webs are gone.

### At the table

Mechanically: "The web's held them two rounds and everyone's clear. Fireball
into the cube. They're Restrained, so Dexterity saves at Disadvantage
against DC 14, 8d6 fire. Anyone who starts a turn in a burning cube takes
2d4 more."

Verbally: *"Kief regards the struggling, silk-wrapped raiders with genuine
regret. 'You had so many chances to be elsewhere.' Then the corridor
breathes in, and blooms."*

### The rule underneath

Conditions chain into save modifiers: Restrained imposes Disadvantage on
Dexterity saves, which directly weakens the enemy's roll against a
Dexterity-save spell. Environments are shared, so allies leave before
ignition. Combo play is reading which clauses touch and in what order.
