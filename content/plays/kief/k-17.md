Kief's attack cantrips have no consolation damage. The doctrine that
replaces it.

### When to use it

- Planning any round where a cantrip is the Action: which cantrip, and
  against which defense?

### Mechanics

1. The gap: a missed *Fire Bolt* or *Ray of Frost* deals nothing. At +6
   against AC 15, the hit chance is 60%.
2. Fix one: **Innate Sorcery**. Advantage raises that to about 84%. Worth
   it when cantrips will carry several rounds of an important fight, but
   there are only two uses per day ([play 45](../k-45/)).
3. Fix two: attack a different defense. [Mind Sliver](../k-16/) uses an
   Intelligence save, which armored brutes often fail.
4. Fix three: stop attacking. *Web*, *Fear*, *Command*, and *Hypnotic
   Pattern* target saves. And when Kief's control spell is already doing
   the work, **Dodge** from cover is a valid turn.

### At the table

Mechanically: "This knight's AC is a problem. Mind Sliver instead, he's
an Intelligence save. If he fails, his next save is minus 1d4, and Mira's
spell goes right after him."

Verbally: *"Kief rolls his shoulders and the ember-scale shimmer wakes
along his forearms. 'Fine,' he says. 'We'll do it the thoughtful way.'"*

### The rule underneath

Know your hit math: chance to hit = (21 + your bonus − target AC) × 5%. At
+6 against AC 15, that's 60%; Advantage lifts it to 1 − 0.4² = 84%.
Advantage is worth the most near a 50% base and never stacks with itself.
Against very high AC, stop making attack rolls. Monsters are rarely strong
in both AC and every save, so read the enemy and pick the weaker defense.
