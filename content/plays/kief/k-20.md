A Wisdom save, or the target spends its next turn doing what Kief said.

### When to use it

- **Drop**: disarm the champion before the party's attack.
- **Flee**: peel one enemy off an ally for a round.
- **Grovel**: knock a melee threat Prone next to the party's fighter.
- **Halt**: freeze the runner going for reinforcements.
- **Approach**: pull an archer out of a good position.

### Mechanics

1. 1st level (Draconic Sorcery, always prepared), Action, range 60 feet:
   one creature Kief can see makes a **Wisdom save against DC 14** (15
   under Innate Sorcery) or follows the command on its **next turn**.
2. The 2024 options: **Approach, Drop, Flee, Grovel, Halt.** Read the full
   spell text at the table for what each one makes the target do.
3. Upcast: one more creature per slot level above 1st. *Drop* at 2nd level
   disarms two guards.
4. Some creatures may be unaffected. Check with the DM whether the target
   can understand the command before relying on it.
5. Subtle Spell (1 SP) makes it invisible in polite company. The social
   version is [play 54](../k-54/).
6. No Concentration. It stacks with Kief's running control spell.

### At the table

Mechanically: "Command on the duelist: 'Drop.' Wisdom save DC 14 or the
sword hits the floor on his turn, and someone can kick it away."

Verbally: *"'Drop,' Kief suggests, in the tone he uses on Mr. Big at
dinnertime, and the duelist's blade rings on the flagstones like a dinner
bell."*

### The rule underneath

Single-target save spells live and die on target selection: aim a Wisdom
save at low-Wisdom brutes, not priests. The effect happens on the target's
own turn, so initiative order decides when the payoff lands. A command
given after the target has already acted this round waits a full round. A
non-Concentration control spell is valuable precisely because it layers
on top of *Web* or *Hypnotic Pattern*.
