Change one enemy’s next turn: one useful opening, then room for the party to act.

### When to use it

One enemy’s next turn matters, and you want an intervention that leaves your existing concentration intact.

### Mechanics

**Opening.** Cast *Command*: Action, one 1st-level slot, a creature you can see within 60 feet, Wisdom save DC 14 (15 with Innate Sorcery). Choose Approach, Drop, Flee, Grovel, or Halt. Name the result you want: a dropped object, a prone target, movement, or a pause.

**Follow-through.** After a failed save, coordinate around the target’s next turn, when the command takes effect. Let a teammate act on the opening when initiative permits; reclaiming a dropped item or attacking still uses the relevant normal actions.

**Fallback.** On a successful save, keep any existing concentration running. On a later turn use a cantrip, an objective action, or a different tool if the situation calls for it. One resisted command does not require another slot.

**Who gets the next moment?** Ask who can benefit from the particular command and its timing. Nearby melee allies may value Grovel; someone pursuing an object may value Drop.

### At the table

Mechanical call: “Command: Drop. Wisdom save. If it fails, they drop what they’re holding and end their next turn.”

Kief’s line: “Management would like you to put that down.”

### The rule underneath

Use the five commands in the 2024 spell, not an invented instruction. It has no concentration and no shared-language or Undead exclusion. Grovel makes the target Prone and ends its turn; attack Advantage against a prone creature requires being within 5 feet. Subtle costs 1 SP if needed, but it does not hide the spell’s consequences.
