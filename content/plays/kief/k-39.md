The enemy caster is holding something nasty. A cantrip that pressures the
concentration.

### When to use it

- An enemy is concentrating on a spell hurting the party: a charm, a
  wall, a summon, an invisible ally.
- *Counterspell* is too late; the spell is already running.
- The caster's Intelligence save looks weaker than their AC.

### Mechanics

1. *Mind Sliver*: Intelligence save against DC 14 (15 under Innate
   Sorcery). Failure: **2d6 psychic** and **−1d4** on its next save before
   the end of Kief's next turn.
2. Taking damage forces a **Constitution save to keep concentrating**: DC
   10 or half the damage, whichever is higher.
3. Whether the −1d4 applies to that same concentration save is a timing
   question. Ask the DM once and note the ruling. If it doesn't, the
   penalty still waits for the next save, so the next hit from an ally
   gets the benefit.
4. Every separate instance of damage forces another save. Several small
   hits from the party beat one big hit for breaking concentration.
5. Alternative: [Fire Bolt](../k-6/) or [Ray of Frost](../k-8/) if the
   target's AC is the weaker defense.

### At the table

Mechanically: "Mind Sliver on the priest holding the charm. Intelligence
DC 14. If it fails, 2d6 psychic, and that's a concentration check. Does
his minus 1d4 apply to it? Either way, Tam, hit him next."

Verbally: *"Kief frowns at the priest the way he frowns at a blend that
won't settle. Something in the priest's attention slips."*

### The rule underneath

Concentration saves are triggered per instance of damage. That makes many
small hits better than one large hit for breaking a spell. Save penalties
that apply to "the next saving throw" interact with whatever save comes
next, including one triggered by the same spell. When rules are ambiguous,
get a ruling once and apply it consistently.
