He cannot attack. He can make someone else's attack better, at a real
price.

### When to use it

- A pivotal attack wants Advantage: the rogue's big strike, the finishing
  blow on a fleeing boss.
- Only when the benefit justifies exposing a 2-HP familiar.

### Mechanics

1. **Help (attack)**: on his own turn, Mr. Big distracts an enemy **within 5
   feet of him**. The next attack roll by one of his allies against that
   enemy has **Advantage**. The benefit expires at the start of Mr. Big's
   next turn.
2. **Help (ability check)**: he can assist a check only with a skill or
   tool he's proficient in: **Perception** or **Stealth**.
3. Timing is initiative. Help works only if the ally attacks between Mr.
   Big's turn and his next one.
4. Don't Help an attack that already has Advantage. It doesn't stack.
5. Risk: he ends his turn next to an armed enemy. A ledge or tabletop beside
   the target keeps him slightly safer.

### At the table

Mechanically: "It's the last exchange. Mr. Big steps onto the mantel beside
the warlord. Help action: Brenna's next attack against him has Advantage.
She's right after him in initiative."

Verbally: *"The warlord glances left at the impossible small cow on the
mantelpiece, one heartbeat of pure bafflement, and Brenna's blade arrives
in the gap."*

### The rule underneath

The 2024 Help action has two modes with different requirements: attack
help needs the enemy within 5 feet of the helper, and check help requires
proficiency in the skill. Because Advantage doesn't stack, Help is wasted
on an attack that already has it. A familiar's Help is a whole action
gifted to the party and priced by the familiar's exposure.
