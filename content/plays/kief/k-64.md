A tiny, silent, entirely serious cow sitting on the counter. The icebreaker
of the party.

### When to use it

- Opening a social scene softer: children, merchants, lonely sentries,
  suspicious farmers.
- Establishing harmlessness before Persuasion goes to work.
- Not in hostile courts where a Fey familiar would be recognized as
  magical and a liability.

### Mechanics

1. Mr. Big stares silently; Kief interprets. The actual Persuasion check and
   the NPC's reaction are the DM's call. There's no free charm effect.
2. The double act: Kief consults him mid-negotiation
   ([play 55](../k-55/)), serves him first at meals, and lets
   counterparties fill the silence.
3. Anyone using *Detect Magic* will see him for what he is: magical, and
   Fey. Enemies who know Kief may target the cow.
4. He is **he**, a colleague, and the senior partner. Never luggage.

### At the table

Mechanically: "I set Mr. Big on the counter facing the merchant and start
the tea. By the time I ask about the shipment, she's been maintaining eye
contact with a tiny cow for two full minutes. Persuasion, plus six."

Verbally: *"'He likes you,' Kief says, which changes nothing and
everything. The merchant looks into the small dark eyes of Mr. Big and
begins, helplessly, to be reasonable."*

### The rule underneath

Circumstances the DM can see in the fiction are the legitimate way to
influence a social DC. Props work that way, and the DM decides how much.
Detection cuts the other way: familiars are magical creatures, and any
effect that senses magic will notice. Every social asset has a detection
surface; know yours.
