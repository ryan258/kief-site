*Fire Bolt* can target objects, and it ignites flammables. A 120-foot
lighter.

### When to use it

- Cut a rope bridge, light a distant brazier, ignite spilled oil, burn
  away webs or vines, from range.
- Any time the environment hurts the enemy more than 2d10 would.
- Never into Kief's own *Web* until the party is clear ([play 66](../k-66/)).

### Mechanics

1. Ranged spell attack against the object (+6). The DM sets the object's
   AC and HP, and may call an unattended target an automatic hit.
2. 2d10 fire to the object. A flammable object that isn't worn or carried
   starts burning.
3. Objects have HP and sometimes resist damage: a rope is a turn, a
   drawbridge is a plan.
4. Warn allies what will be on fire next round. Kief has no fire
   resistance either.

### At the table

Mechanically: "I'm not shooting the ogre. I'm shooting the tar barrel next
to it. Fire Bolt, 2d10 to the barrel, and it's alight."

Verbally: *"Kief considers the ogre, then the barrel, the way he'd choose
between loose leaf and bagged. He chooses the barrel."*

### The rule underneath

Spells that say they can affect objects mean it. Fire Bolt's ignition
clause, "if it isn't being worn or carried," is the load-bearing text:
carried objects are protected. Environmental damage bypasses the monster's
AC; when the rope, bridge, or barrel is the real target, aim at it and say
why.
