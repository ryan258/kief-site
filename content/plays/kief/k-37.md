Kief has three control spells that compete for the same Concentration.
Choosing correctly matters more than rolling well.

### When to use it

- Round one of a fight that needs control, before the slot is spent.

### Mechanics

1. **Hypnotic Pattern** (3rd): enemies **stay put**, helpless, and wake on
   damage. Best when the party wants time: to deal with the rest, to tie
   them up, or to leave. Useless against charm-immune enemies or ones that
   can't see.
2. **Fear** (3rd): enemies **leave** and drop what they're holding. Best
   when the party wants space, or wants weapons on the floor. Allows
   Opportunity Attacks as they flee; runners may return with friends.
3. **Web** (2nd): enemies **slow down** and some are Restrained, giving
   Advantage to attackers. Best in doorways and corridors, and it saves a
   3rd-level slot. Allies share the terrain.
4. Ask three questions: Is anything immune? Where are the allies? Does the
   party want these enemies **still**, **gone**, or **stuck**?
5. Careful Spell (1 SP) protects up to three chosen creatures against
   Pattern or Fear. Its Web interaction is pending; web terrain still applies.
6. Keep a 3rd-level slot for [Counterspell](../k-30/) if a caster is
   present. Web is the better opener then.

### At the table

Mechanically: "Undead with no eyes? Pattern won't work. They're in a
corridor, so Web: Dexterity DC 14, and I keep my 3rd-level slots."

Verbally: *"Kief considers the room the way he considers a shelf of teas:
this one calms, this one sends you home early, and this one makes
everything stick."*

### The rule underneath

Control spells differ by save (Wisdom versus Dexterity), by condition
(Charmed, Frightened, Restrained), and by what ends them. Condition
immunities in a stat block silently turn a spell off, so read them before
casting. The best control spell is the one whose end condition the enemy
can't easily trigger and the party won't trigger by accident.
