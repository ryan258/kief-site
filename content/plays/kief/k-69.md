Out of reach and in range. The selfish plan: both 3rd-level slots and
Kief's Concentration.

### When to use it

- Open-field fights against grounded melee enemies with no ranged answer.
- The party wants Kief out of reach more than it wants his control spells.
- Rarely. It spends everything big Kief has on one fight.

### Mechanics

1. **Turn 1**: [Fly](../k-32/) on himself, Concentration. Move up to 60 feet,
   out of reach and outside javelin range if possible.
2. **Turn 2**: [Fireball](../k-28/) on the cluster, 150 feet of range, with
   Careful if allies are inside. That's the second 3rd-level slot; there is
   no *Counterspell* left.
3. **Failure modes**: ranged attackers (altitude isn't cover; stay far and
   ready *Shield*), and **losing Concentration**, which drops Kief
   1d6 per 10 feet.
4. **Safety line**: if Fly ends while he's aloft, *Feather Fall* on himself
   as a Reaction, if the Reaction is unspent. Stay below 60 feet over
   uncertain ground.
5. No Web, Pattern, or Fear for the rest of the fight while Fly holds.
6. Later turns: cantrips from altitude while the minutes run.

### At the table

Mechanically: "Fly on me, concentrating. I go up forty feet over the river,
out of reach. Next turn, Fireball on the shield wall: Dexterity DC 14, 8d6.
My Reaction stays free for Feather Fall in case they knock me out of the
sky."

Verbally: *"The war band looks up into the noon glare and finds a small
silhouette hanging there, patient as a kettle. The first sphere of fire is
already on its way down."*

### The rule underneath

High ground isn't a printed bonus in 2024: altitude's real mechanics are
reach and range. Melee can't reach a flyer, but ranged attacks can, and
Concentration loss turns height into falling damage. An exposure trade
needs its own insurance, and a Reaction held for Feather Fall is exactly
that.
