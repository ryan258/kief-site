One word, no visible casting. Enchantment as etiquette, and a serious
choice.

### When to use it

- A negotiation turning violent: "Halt" on the guard reaching for the bell,
  "Drop" on the hand going for the dagger.
- Extractions and arrests that must not become brawls.
- **Not** for winning ordinary arguments. Magical coercion discovered later
  has consequences, and the table may reasonably treat it as a hostile act.

### Mechanics

1. Subtle Spell (1 SP) + *Command* (1st-level slot): Wisdom save against DC
   14, and no Verbal or Somatic components to see.
2. The options: Approach, Drop, Flee, Grovel, Halt. Social scenes mostly
   want **Halt** and **Drop**.
3. A target that saves may feel something. From across a crowded room,
   suspicion has nowhere to land; from across the tea table, choose the
   moment carefully.
4. It lasts for the target's next turn only. The follow-up is the
   conversation.
5. Uses the same 1 SP that *Careful* would need later in the day.

### At the table

Mechanically: "The captain's reaching for the alarm horn. Subtle Spell, one
point, Command: 'Halt.' Wisdom save DC 14. As far as this room knows, he
just paused to think."

Verbally: *"The captain stops, hand an inch from the horn, suddenly unsure
why. Kief pours a second cup. 'You look like a man reconsidering,' he
says, approvingly."*

### The rule underneath

A casting with no components can't be perceived or identified as it
happens. Its effect can still be noticed, and a failed save leaves the DM
to decide what the target understands. Social magic is a question of
table trust as much as rules: agree with the DM what these spells mean in
this setting before using them on people the party must keep living with.
