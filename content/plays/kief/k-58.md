Declare intent and method, never outcomes. Every other play runs through
this one.

### When to use it

- Every check, every scene, every spell.

### Mechanics

1. ❌ "I convince the guard." ✅ "Kief offers a cup and suggests, slowly and
   warmly, that nobody needs paperwork tonight. Persuasion?"
2. ❌ "Mr. Big finds the trap." ✅ "Mr. Big walks the corridor ahead, low,
   stopping at anything that looks wrong. I'm listening for his report."
3. Magic gets the same discipline, **before dice**: spell, level, targets,
   Metamagic. "Fireball, 3rd, centered behind the ogre, Careful on Brenna
   and Tam, one Sorcery Point."
4. **Mechanics first, then the drawl.** Make the combat decision promptly;
   add one Kief sentence afterward. The joke is his delivery; the party
   depends on his decisions.
5. Share information immediately. Ask before putting an ally on a flight
   plan.

### At the table

Mechanically: The pattern is always the same: what Kief wants, what Kief
does, which number applies. Then the dice, if the DM still wants them.

Verbally: *"Kief operates the way he brews: exact leaves, exact water,
exact time. Then he lets the cup do the arguing."*

### The rule underneath

This is the resolution loop itself: the DM describes, you declare intent
and method, and the DM resolves, using dice only where outcomes are
uncertain and matter. Specific methods select the check, the modifiers,
and sometimes success without a roll. You can't roll your way out of an
unspecified plan, and with a specified one you often don't need to roll.
