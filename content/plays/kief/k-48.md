Careful or Subtle, never both on one spell at level 5. Choosing between
them is the play.

### When to use it

- Any casting where both options look tempting: a *Fireball* near allies
  in front of an enemy caster, a *Hypnotic Pattern* in a crowded room
  where nobody should see Kief cast.

### Mechanics

1. The base rule: **one Metamagic option per spell**. Sorcery Incarnate,
   which lifts that limit, arrives at level 7.
2. **Careful** (1 SP) wins when allies are inside the area and would take
   damage or lose their turn.
3. **Subtle** (1 SP) wins when being seen casting is the bigger danger: an
   enemy counterspeller, a courtroom, a guard post, a gag.
4. Can't have both? Change the geometry instead. Move the *Fireball* point
   so allies are outside, and then Subtle is free to use. Or cast from out
   of sight, and then Careful is the only option you need.
5. Kief has only **5 SP**. Metamagic on every spell empties the battery by
   the second fight.

### At the table

Mechanically: "I'd love this Pattern to be both Careful and Subtle, but I
can't yet. Brenna, step back five feet out of the cube, and I'll cast it
Subtle so their mage has nothing to counter."

Verbally: *"Kief weighs the options, one hand after the other, like two
teas of nearly equal merit. 'Can't have everything,' he admits. 'Yet.'"*

### The rule underneath

Metamagic is a menu, not a stack: unless a feature says otherwise, each
spell takes one option. Limits like this reward positioning, because many
problems Metamagic solves can also be solved by where you stand or where
you aim. Knowing when a feature arrives (Sorcery Incarnate, level 7) keeps
old higher-level tactics from sneaking into current play.
