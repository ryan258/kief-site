The answer to resistances, if the campaign budget includes a diamond.
Ownership is **pending**. Without it, this play does not exist.

### When to use it

- The enemy resists fire or cold, and single-target slot damage is worth
  it.
- Enemies within 30 feet of each other, where a leap might carry on.
- Only after a 50+ GP diamond is confirmed in Kief's inventory.

### Mechanics

1. 1st level (Draconic Sorcery, always prepared), Action, range 90 feet,
   ranged spell attack **+6** (Advantage under Innate Sorcery). Choose
   acid, cold, fire, lightning, poison, or thunder: **3d8**.
2. **The orb leaps**: if two or more of the d8s show the same number, it
   leaps to a different creature within 30 feet of the target: new attack
   roll, new damage. Maximum leaps equal the slot level, and each creature
   can be targeted only once per casting.
3. Upcast: +1d8 per slot level above 1st, and more leaps. At 3rd level:
   5d8, up to three leaps. That competes with *Counterspell* and
   *Hypnotic Pattern* for a scarce slot.
4. No Elemental Affinity bonus at level 5.
5. Component: a diamond worth **50+ GP**, not consumed. Subtle Spell cannot
   remove it.

### At the table

Mechanically: "Fire-immune, wonderful. Chromatic Orb, lightning, 1st level:
17 to hit, 3d8 lightning. Two fours on the dice, so it leaps to the cultist
behind him: new attack roll."

Verbally: *"Kief produces a small diamond, and the orb that condenses
around it cycles through colors like steeping tea deciding what it wants to
be. It settles on storm."*

### The rule underneath

Costed material components must be owned, but unless the text says
"consumes," they survive every casting: one diamond powers unlimited orbs.
Subtle Spell can skip ordinary components, never priced or consumed ones.
The leap reads the damage dice as a trigger, so upcasting adds dice and
also raises the odds of a match.
