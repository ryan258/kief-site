Kief's real tool proficiency is calligraphy, not herbalism. A merchant's
skill with labels, ledgers, and letters.

### When to use it

- Writing something that must look official, careful, or trustworthy: a
  letter of introduction, a merchant's label, a signed agreement.
- Examining handwriting or a document's craftsmanship, if the DM allows the
  tool to help.
- Copying a document accurately for the party.

### Mechanics

1. **Calligrapher's Supplies**, proficient from Sage. Dexterity-based by
   default: **+5** (Dexterity +2, proficiency +3). The DM may choose
   another ability for a particular task.
2. Describe the goal and the method; the DM sets the DC.
3. Forgery is a different, darker request. Ask the DM whether calligraphy
   applies, and expect consequences if it's discovered.
4. The supplies themselves need to be in the pack. Starting gear is
   pending.
5. The **herbalism kit** is desired character equipment, but Kief is **not
   proficient**. Checks with it are plain ability checks.

### At the table

Mechanically: "I write a proper letter of introduction to the guild
factor, in merchant's hand with the Leaf & Ledger's usual flourishes.
Calligrapher's Supplies, plus five, if a check's needed."

Verbally: *"Kief's letters are unhurried, generous with their loops, and
perfectly legible. The ink smells faintly of bergamot. The factor reads it
twice and sits up straighter."*

### The rule underneath

Tool proficiency is separate from skill proficiency. With proficiency, you
add your bonus to checks using the tool; without it, the check is just the
ability modifier. The DM chooses the ability that fits the task. Knowing
which tools your character is actually proficient with prevents the
common drift of a character's story outgrowing its sheet.
