Current local build; see [the character record](/character/). Kief is starting a new campaign. His job is to make everybody else's turn better and make sure the party gets home.

## The normal fight

1. Identify the problem: a crowd, a choke point, a caster, or an ally in trouble.
2. In an important fight, activate Innate Sorcery with your Bonus Action. Cast Web or Hypnotic Pattern with your Action if control fits the situation. These can happen on the same turn.
3. Tell the party what changed: “Those three are out. Hit the one on the left.”
4. Preserve concentration from cover. Mind Sliver supports an ally's next saving-throw effect; Ray of Frost slows pursuit. Dodge is a good turn when your existing spell is doing the work.
5. Spend another big slot only when it solves a new problem. You have just two third-level slots.

Hypnotic Pattern has a charm limitation; switch plans against immune creatures. Web is useful control at a cheaper slot, but allies also face its terrain and saves. Fire can destroy the webbing; don't sabotage your own battlefield control casually. Fireball is the damage fallback, with Careful if allies need protection.

## Make the team shine

**For the martial:** Web can restrain targets, giving attacks against them advantage. Command can interrupt a dangerous foe. Mr. Big may Help when the benefit justifies exposing him.

**For the caster:** Mind Sliver can penalize the target's next save by 1d4 if the initial Intelligence save fails. Coordinate timing: another save can consume that penalty first. Counterspell may stop the enemy's most dangerous cast.

**For the scout:** Cast Invisibility on them and let them lead. Stay somewhere safe enough to maintain concentration. Agree on a return signal; invisibility does not remove noise or tracks.

**For the explorer:** Light, Mage Hand, Mending, Detect Magic, Speak with Animals, and Mr. Big cover many mundane problems without burning the day's big slots. Fly solves a specific obstacle; rope can keep solving it after the spell ends.

**For the wounded:** Help them get out of danger. Feather Fall is fall protection, not general damage prevention. There is no healing spell on this build; obtain mundane first aid or potions if the campaign budget allows.

## Reaction discipline

Shield protects you; Feather Fall protects falling allies; Counterspell contests a spell. Once one is used, the others are unavailable until your next turn begins. If an enemy caster is the main threat, keep line of sight, stay within 60 feet when feasible, and leave a third-level slot available. Don't spend every slot on an opening spectacle.

## Resource rhythm

Start with 5 SP. Careful and Subtle each cost 1. Don't convert your last defensive first-level slot reflexively. Turning all 5 SP into a third-level slot is possible, but spends the entire Metamagic reserve. A Short Rest can return up to 2 spent SP once per Long Rest; ask for it when the group also benefits.

Concentration is the real bottleneck. Fly on the fighter ends Web. Invisibility ends Hypnotic Pattern. Say the tradeoff aloud before making it, especially if an ally is airborne.

## Roleplay without slowing the table

Describe the result in plain mechanics, then add one Kief sentence. Make the combat decision promptly; drawl afterward.

- “Mind Sliver on the captain; if it lands, his next save gets -1d4. Mate, your thoughts have got knots in them.”
- “Feather Fall on the falling party members. Easy. The ground can wait.”
- “Invisibility on our scout. You're going to have to trust that I'm making eye contact.”
- “Careful Pattern; these three allies are protected. Everyone else, take a little moment.”
- “Mr. Big scouts the next room. Management would like to inspect the premises.”

Share information immediately. Ask before putting an ally on a flight plan. Keep the blends mysterious, but keep your turns clear. The cow's opinion is flavor, not a veto over the rest of the party.

## Build tradeoffs

Careful keeps control and emergency damage usable around friends. Subtle keeps the quiet, improbable problem-solving personality and helps with component restrictions. The original Constitution 18 / Charisma 16 remains: sturdy concentration, somewhat lower save DC than a fresh maximum-Charisma build. The utility preparations replace redundant damage/control options, while subclass spells supply Fly and Fear for free.

This is a versatile teammate, not a healer or an answer to every obstacle. Revisit the spell list after seeing what the other characters actually cover.
