## Six signature plays

Use these as familiar choices; keep the twelve-play index above for emergencies and variety. Each sequence has a next step, a fallback, and a teammate handoff. Current build unchanged.

### Give the party breathing room

**Use when.** Several dangerous enemies fit in a 30-foot cube within 120 feet and can see the pattern. Consider known Charmed immunity; creature type alone does not decide it.

**Opening.** In an important fight, activate Innate Sorcery with your Bonus Action if available. Cast *Hypnotic Pattern* with your Action: one 3rd-level slot, concentration up to 1 minute, Wisdom save DC 14 (15 with Innate Sorcery). Use Careful for 1 SP if up to three allies need protection; name them before saves.

**Follow-through.** Name the enemies actually affected after saves resolve. Move somewhere safer, then on later turns use *Ray of Frost* or *Fire Bolt* against an unaffected threat, Dodge, or take an appropriate action toward the objective. Keep damage away from affected creatures.

**Fallback.** If few enemies fail, use the opening you did get. If none fail, choose a cantrip or *Command* on a later turn, or *Web* if its placement solves the problem. Spend the other 3rd-level slot only for a new urgent need.

**Who gets the next moment?** Ask the party to choose a target order or an escape route. The team decides what to do with the pause.

Mechanical call: “Hypnotic Pattern here; Careful protects these allies. These enemies failed. Focus on the guard by the door.”

Kief’s line: “Let’s all take a moment. Them especially.”

**Boundary.** Affected creatures are Charmed, Incapacitated, and Speed 0, not Unconscious. Damage or someone else’s Action shaking a creature ends its effect. You have two 3rd-level slots. Careful and Subtle cannot be combined on this build.

### Make the doorway a problem

**Use when.** A supported doorway, corridor, or route can hold enemies away from the party. Allies have a way to stay outside the webs.

**Opening.** Cast *Web*: Action, one 2nd-level slot, range 60 feet, 20-foot cube, concentration up to 1 hour. Anchor it between solid masses or layer it over a floor, wall, or ceiling. Announce the area and the route allies should use.

**Follow-through.** On later turns, attack an enemy that actually becomes Restrained, slow an approaching enemy with *Ray of Frost*, or Dodge from a safer position. Ask allies to exploit the opening while keeping their own route clear.

**Fallback.** If enemies avoid the webs, use the route they chose to protect the objective or withdraw. If a target succeeds, it still faces the difficult terrain. If placement offers no useful control, use *Command* or a cantrip on a later turn rather than forcing another Web.

**Who gets the next moment?** Tell the party which enemies are Restrained and which route is clear. Invite allies to choose where to focus attacks.

Mechanical call: “Web across this route. Saves happen on first entry on a turn or at turn start there. Keep our side clear.”

Kief’s line: “The room would like you to slow down.”

**Boundary.** There is no immediate save merely for casting Web. First entry on a turn or starting a turn there triggers a Dexterity save, DC 14 (15 with Innate Sorcery). The area is difficult terrain and Lightly Obscured. Restraint lasts while in the webs or until breaking free with an Action and Strength (Athletics) check against the DC. Fire burns webbing away; avoid sabotaging the control. Careful’s delayed/repeated-save interaction is pending; keep allies outside.

### Keep the opening alive

**Use when.** Your concentration spell is already helping. Name what it is buying: a safe approach, a blocked route, a paused enemy, or an ally’s flight.

**Opening.** Move to a safer position without inviting avoidable opportunity attacks. Choose one useful Action: *Ray of Frost* slows a pursuer on a hit; *Fire Bolt* damages a suitable target; Dodge protects you; an objective action helps the party finish the encounter.

**Follow-through.** Recheck the spell’s purpose each turn. With *Hypnotic Pattern*, leave affected enemies undamaged. With *Web*, keep allies outside and avoid burning it. With *Fly*, keep the recipient’s landing in mind. With *Invisibility*, let the scout lead and watch the return plan.

**Fallback.** If concentration breaks, announce it immediately so allies can adapt. Protect an airborne ally with *Feather Fall* only if its trigger, range, Reaction, and slot are available. On your next turn, choose the current problem rather than automatically recasting the lost spell.

**Who gets the next moment?** Say what you are maintaining and ask which objective or remaining threat needs your help. A quiet turn can preserve another player’s big moment.

Mechanical call: “I’m keeping the spell running. I’ll take cover and Dodge while you get them through.”

Kief’s line: “Everybody breathe. The difficult bit is still working.”

**Boundary.** One concentration spell at a time; starting another ends the first. Ritual casting also requires concentration while casting. After damage, make a Constitution save at +7: DC 10 or half damage rounded down, whichever is higher, maximum DC 30. Shield is AC 20 until your next turn starts; ordinary totals 15–19 become misses, 20 still hits, and critical hits remain hits. Shield, Counterspell, and Feather Fall share one Reaction. Dodge’s attack Disadvantage requires seeing the attacker and ends if you are Incapacitated or your Speed is 0.

### Change one enemy’s next turn

**Use when.** One enemy’s next turn matters, and you want an intervention that leaves your existing concentration intact.

**Opening.** Cast *Command*: Action, one 1st-level slot, a creature you can see within 60 feet, Wisdom save DC 14 (15 with Innate Sorcery). Choose Approach, Drop, Flee, Grovel, or Halt. Name the result you want: a dropped object, a prone target, movement, or a pause.

**Follow-through.** After a failed save, coordinate around the target’s next turn, when the command takes effect. Let a teammate act on the opening when initiative permits; reclaiming a dropped item or attacking still uses the relevant normal actions.

**Fallback.** On a successful save, keep any existing concentration running. On a later turn use a cantrip, an objective action, or a different tool if the situation calls for it. One resisted command does not require another slot.

**Who gets the next moment?** Ask who can benefit from the particular command and its timing. Nearby melee allies may value Grovel; someone pursuing an object may value Drop.

Mechanical call: “Command: Drop. Wisdom save. If it fails, they drop what they’re holding and end their next turn.”

Kief’s line: “Management would like you to put that down.”

**Boundary.** Use the five commands in the 2024 spell, not an invented instruction. It has no concentration and no shared-language or Undead exclusion. Grovel makes the target Prone and ends its turn; attack Advantage against a prone creature requires being within 5 feet. Subtle costs 1 SP if needed, but it does not hide the spell’s consequences.

### Let the scout do their thing

**Use when.** A willing teammate wants concealment for a specific approach or investigation and you can safely support their return.

**Opening.** Ask for their route, goal, return time or signal, and fallback meeting place. Cast *Invisibility*: Action, touch, one 2nd-level slot, concentration up to 1 hour. Announce any concentration spell you are replacing before casting.

**Follow-through.** Stay somewhere safe enough to maintain concentration. Let the scout make their own decisions and required checks. When they return, ask for the key observation and invite the party to decide the next move.

**Fallback.** If the spell ends or the scout cannot proceed, use the agreed meeting place. A visible scout can still retreat or use ordinary cover. For a physical gap rather than concealment, consider *Fly* with a landing plan; it replaces Invisibility.

**Who gets the next moment?** The scout owns the approach and report. Kief supplies the opportunity, not instructions for every footstep.

Mechanical call: “Invisibility on our scout. I’ll hold concentration here; come back to this spot if the plan changes.”

Kief’s line: “Lovely. I’ve misplaced you already.”

**Boundary.** Invisibility ends for a target that attacks, deals damage, or casts a spell. It does not silence footsteps, remove tracks, or guarantee stealth. Kief must touch the target; familiar delivery depends on the DM retaining that mechanic. Ending Fly can cause a fall, so settle the landing before switching concentration.

### The Syrup Witness

**Use when.** A witness describes a confusing chase or encounter and the party would benefit from examining the same account.

**Opening.** Ask: “Can I illustrate your account in this basin so we can question it together?” Use *Shape Water* on visible liquid within 30 feet, fitting in a 5-foot cube. Form simple shapes and animate the account, one movement at a time; invite corrections.

**Follow-through.** Let the witness correct your picture, then invite a teammate to ask the next question. Repeat a casting only when another listed effect is needed. Finish by stating the part the party agrees it heard.

**Fallback.** If the liquid or proposed effect does not fit, use an ordinary sketch with available materials or *Minor Illusion* for a static object image within its limits. If the testimony conflicts, note the disagreement and ask another witness; the picture cannot settle truth.

**Who gets the next moment?** A teammate chooses the next question or compares the account with an existing clue. Kief makes the conversation easier to share.

Mechanical call: “Shape Water to illustrate what the witness describes. Please correct the picture before we continue.”

Kief’s line: “The tall lump is the duke. Please interrupt if I have made him too flattering.”

**Boundary.** Shape Water uses an Action per casting, no slot and no concentration. The DM-approved liquid scope is recorded; hardening unusual candy liquids is still unspecified. A lasting shape effect uses one of the two allowed active non-instantaneous effects and lasts 1 hour. This illustrates testimony; it does not retrieve memories, prove truth, create creatures, or grant automatic Advantage. Minor Illusion’s object image is static, not an animated replacement.

The DM encourages wit and whimsy and brief, varied contributions. Make one small proposal and accept the ruling; detailed spell-use history remains in [Table rulings](/rulings/#campaign-direction-wit-and-whimsy).

## What Candy Land will keep asking for

The road is the campaign, so plays 3, 4, 5, 6, and 10 come up more than the set-piece ones. Following the map means scouting (5), crossing what the map does not mention (6), and catching whoever the map drops (4). Half-forgotten stories mean asking animals what they saw (10) and inspecting the thing that is too magical to be decorative (11).

Before committing a third-level slot, check the creature's actual condition immunities. Hypnotic Pattern (1) relies on Charmed. Fear applies Frightened and also makes a creature that fails its save drop what it is holding, so resolve that spell text separately. Fey, Undead, and constructs have no blanket immunity from their type alone. Web (3), Fireball (9), and 2024 Command use their own requirements rather than a Charm/Fear gate.

For the map and old stories, let the clues steep: History +2 compares names and accounts, Arcana +2 examines magical traces, Insight +3 reads a speaker, and Persuasion +6 opens a conversation. Ask who last saw the King, who is repeating the Princess story, and which local actually has authority. Speak with Animals adds animal witnesses with animal-sized points of view. Checks and clues are the DM's call; Kief has no Investigation, Nature, Survival, or Herbalism Kit proficiency, so invite the party's specialists into those parts of the mystery.

## The rules underneath

Concentration is one ongoing spell at a time. Two third-level slots means the party cannot receive every big play in one fight. Pattern ends on a creature when it takes damage or someone uses an action to shake it awake; mark targets and focus the remaining threats. Careful does not remove Web terrain; its delayed/repeated-save interaction awaits the ruling recorded in [Table rulings](/rulings/). Keep allies outside. No elemental damage bonus at level 5.

Elementalism provides only its listed small effects. A smoke shape does not automatically obscure vision, distract an enemy, or grant advantage; ask the DM to resolve any proposed consequence. An attack cantrip is useful; maintaining a successful control spell while taking cover can be more useful.

Misty Step is the personal exit: Bonus Action, visible space within 30 feet, no passenger. After spending a slot on it, use a cantrip or a non-slot action on that turn. He cannot Dimension Door anybody out yet.

Subtle Spell costs 1 SP and removes components other than priced/consumed materials. Use for quiet utility when justified; it doesn't make spell effects invisible or erase social consequences. It cannot be combined with Careful on this level 5 build.
